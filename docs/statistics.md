# Stage 4 analysis plan

The prespecified family contains 25 two-sided tests: 18 chi-square tests for binary and ordinal predictors, plus seven Mann–Whitney U tests for BMI, mental/physical health days, general health, age, education, and income. H0, H1, statistic, raw p-value, BH-adjusted p-value, effect size, interval, method, status, and interpretation are exported per test. Benjamini–Hochberg correction covers all successfully executed tests together, at a reporting cutoff of 0.05. Skips remain explicit. Correlated predictors and post-hoc exploration limit multiplicity control and interpretation; these are exploratory tests, not confirmatory claims.

Chi-square uses no Yates correction and reports minimum expected count and Cramer's V. Expected cells below five trigger a warning, not an unqualified inference. Binary effects are unadjusted odds ratios for code 1 versus code 0, with 95% log-Wald intervals from statsmodels. Zero cells add 0.5 to every cell for OR estimation only, recorded in the table. For multilevel ordinal predictors the effect is omnibus Cramer's V; its CI is intentionally not estimated (method column explains the blank), rather than reporting an unrelated binary contrast. Such estimates could be added with survey-aware resampling in a later analysis.

Mann–Whitney uses a tie-corrected asymptotic, two-sided test. Its effect is rank-biserial correlation: positive means higher predictor codes among target-positive respondents. The 95% large-sample normal interval uses empirical per-group placement variances, with ties counting as half a win, and is clipped to [-1, 1]. It is approximate and can degenerate for constant or perfectly separated samples; no exact small-sample coverage is claimed. U tests compare distributions, not just medians unless additional distribution-shape assumptions hold.

Intervals are marginal, not multiplicity-adjusted or covariate-adjusted. None of these tests accounts for unavailable survey weights/clusters or establishes causation. Large sample sizes make tiny effects significant. Age, socioeconomic status, healthcare access, and other factors can confound associations; adjusted inference is deferred. The target combines self-reported prediabetes/diabetes, so odds ratios do not describe lab-confirmed disease incidence or diagnostic performance.
