# Stage 2 decisions

Default: retain duplicate rows. There is no respondent identifier, and identical coded profiles need not represent accidental duplicates. `--duplicates drop` is an explicit sensitivity option, not the primary analysis. The report records before/after counts. Either way, repeated profiles and survey design limit the independence assumptions of later tests.

No balancing, winsorizing, scaling, or automatic imputation occurs in stages 1–4. Missing, nonnumeric, fractional or out-of-range codes stop cleaning with a concrete error. Valid source codes are stored as integers. BMI's 12–98 source bounds identify unexpected inputs, not universal clinical plausibility thresholds. Age, education, income, and general health retain their ordinal coding. Health-day variables stay between 0 and 30. Quantiles and distributions expose unusual but valid observations rather than deleting them.

`cleaning.json` also contains `review_flags`: counts of valid but unusual or logically odd responses (told cholesterol was high without a cholesterol check, BMI below 15 or above 50/60, and heaping at 30 days for the health-day variables). They are reported only; no row is altered or removed, because the BMI bounds in the schema are the observed source range and cannot flag implausible values by themselves. The data dictionary is exported with each run. A failed cleaning attempt retains the stage 1 quality report for diagnosis, but does not produce a cleaned analysis table.

The primary combined cohort keeps every respondent; its positive label merges original codes 1 and 2. The secondary prediabetes-only cohort intentionally excludes original target code 2 (existing diabetes) before feature cleaning, and provenance records the 35,346 excluded respondents from the 253,680-row source. That scientific eligibility rule is distinct from missing-value or duplicate handling; all eligible respondents are retained by default.
